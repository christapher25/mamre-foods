## 1. Purpose and Scope
==============================================================================

### 1.1 Business context

Mamre Foods is a small US-based food business. It makes half-cooked chapathi, packs it in packets of 12 (the standard packet), and sells to restaurants, shops, retail and catering customers. Some customers pay once a month, some pay in parts. The business owner (the Admin) reviews everything on a laptop. A field Worker creates invoices on an Android handheld device that has a built-in thermal printer. All money is in US dollars. Tax handling is out of scope.

### 1.2 Goals

1. Create an invoice in the field in under a minute, with or without internet, and print it.
2. Keep every customer's balance correct to the cent, including monthly credit and part-payments.
3. Record customer returns and production damage.
4. Calculate the cost per packet from material purchases, usage and expenses, so nobody has to guess it.
5. Give the Admin a detailed monthly report of sales, expenses and profit.

### 1.3 Non-goals for version 1

Tax or VAT, payroll, multiple currencies, online payment gateways, a customer portal, multi-warehouse inventory, loyalty or discount schemes, route planning, multi-business tenancy, and any feature not written in this document.

### 1.4 Success criteria

- A worker with no signal creates and prints an invoice; it later reaches the Admin exactly once.
- A customer's balance on the printed invoice equals the balance in the Admin ledger.
- The Admin can open any month and see sales, direct cost, indirect expenses and profit.
- A worker can never see cost, profit, expense or report data, on screen or in the API.

## 3. Products
==============================================================================

Two products at launch, both sold half-cooked. Adding a new product must be a simple Admin form with no code change.

| Product | Description | Standard packet |
|---|---|---|
| Mamre Fresh Chapathi | Half-cooked chapathi, no preservative | 12 chapathis (editable) |
| Mamre Chapathi | Half-cooked chapathi with potassium sorbate as preservative | 12 chapathis (editable) |

- Product fields: name, code, standard packet size, yield per kg of wheat (32), active flag. Packing is a material (section 9), not a product field.
- The sales unit is the packet. A packet is standard (12 chapathis, A-19) or custom (1 to 200 chapathis, available for every customer type). Each invoice line stores its packets and the chapathis per packet, so one invoice can mix sizes of the same product on separate lines (A-1).
- A new product can be sold as soon as it has prices. Its cost shows as incomplete until a recipe exists.

## 4. Customers and Pricing
==============================================================================

### 4.1 Customer types and customers

Customer types: Restaurant, Shop, Retail and Catering. The Admin may add types. A saved customer has: name, type, phone, address, payment mode (Cash or Credit), optional opening balance, notes, active flag. A walk-in sale is an anonymous Retail sale with no ledger; it follows the Retail price list and the Retail price-edit setting, and must be paid in full at the time of sale. Only the Admin creates saved customers (A-13). A customer the Admin adds or edits reaches every worker's app at its next sync.

### 4.2 Price resolution order

1. An active override price for that customer and product (set by Admin, used for special or discounted prices).
2. Otherwise the default price for that customer type and product.
3. Otherwise no price: the product cannot be invoiced. The app shows "No price set - contact admin". Never default to zero.

Price of a custom packet (unless changed by the worker where allowed) = list price per standard packet x N chapathis / standard packet size, rounded half up, integer arithmetic only. Examples: 250 cents per standard packet of 12 gives 208 cents for 10 chapathis and 188 cents for 9 (a half rounds up); 230 cents gives 192 cents for 10 chapathis.

### 4.3 Price rules

- Prices carry an effective-from date. History is kept. A new price for a type and product needs a later effective date than the current one.
- Each invoice line stores a snapshot of the unit price charged and the list price at that time. Later price changes never alter existing invoices.
- Worker price editing (amends A-2). Each customer type has a switch "Worker can edit price", set by the Admin, change-logged and synced to workers. Default: ON for Retail and Catering, OFF for Restaurant and Shop. When ON, the worker may set the price per packet on any line (above $0 and at most 10 times the list price, as a typing guard). When OFF the price is read-only.
- A changed price is stored with the list price and a flag price_overridden. The Admin sees "List $2.60, charged $2.47" on the invoice and a "Price changed" filter. The receipt shows only the charged price.
- Discounts for a regular customer are still done as an Admin-set override price.

>> [NOTE]
> Risk. Where price editing is on, a worker could undercharge and keep the difference. The Admin should review the Price changed list regularly. A minimum price per product is an open question (P-11).

## 5. Invoicing
==============================================================================

### 5.1 Worker flow

1. Choose the customer type (Restaurant, Shop, Retail, Catering), then the customer, or Walk-in under Retail.
2. Add products. For each line choose Standard (12) or a Custom packet (1 to 200 chapathis) and the number of packets. Prices and the total appear automatically; the price can be changed only where the type allows it.
3. Payment step: amount received now (may be $0 for credit customers) and method.
4. Worker confirms. The invoice is saved on the device, printed, and queued for sync.

### 5.2 Invoice number

Format MAM-<DEVICE>-<SEQ>, for example MAM-W1-0042. The device code (W1, W2...) belongs to one worker and device. The sequence is assigned on the device, increases by one, and is never reused. This prevents number clashes when several devices sync after being offline.

### 5.3 What an invoice shows

- Business name (and address and phone once supplied, P-6), invoice number, date and time, worker, customer name and type.
- Line items: product, packets, unit price charged, line total. A custom packet shows "(N pcs)". Invoice total.
- Payment received at this visit (amount and method) and balance after this invoice.
- For credit customers, a month summary: balance brought forward, invoiced this month, credits from returns, each payment this month (date, amount, method), and total due.

### 5.4 Rules

- A confirmed invoice cannot be edited. Mistakes are fixed by an Admin void (reason required; the number stays and shows VOID) or by a return credit.
- Reprinting is allowed from Today's invoices and is marked DUPLICATE COPY.
- A failed print never loses the invoice. It is saved first, then printed, and can be reprinted.
- Receipts: when a worker confirms a payment, a receipt prints. A payment with no new invoice (customer pays old balance) also prints a receipt (A-16).

==============================================================================
## 6. Payments and Customer Ledger
==============================================================================

### 6.1 Payment methods and customer modes

- Methods accepted: Cash, Zelle, Check, Card, Other (with a note) (A-3).
- Cash customers pay at the time of sale. Credit customers may leave a balance and are billed per calendar month in the business time zone (P-7).
- This applies equally to restaurants, shops and named retail customers.

### 6.2 Partial payments and allocation

- A customer may pay any amount. Several part-payments in a month are normal.
- Each payment is applied to the oldest unpaid invoice first, spilling into the next as needed. Allocation rows are stored. The server performs allocation when records sync.
- An overpayment becomes credit on account (negative balance), shown as such (A-4).

### 6.3 Balance

>> [RULE]
> Customer balance = opening balance + non-void invoices - credits from returns - payments. It is always calculated from records. It is never a typed or editable number.

### 6.4 Month summary and month lock

For any customer and month the system can show: opening balance, invoiced, credits, each payment (date, amount, method), and closing balance. The Admin can lock a month after review. A locked month accepts no back-dated entries; corrections go into the current month (A-5).

### 6.5 Worked example (used as a test case)

A credit restaurant starts October with a $0.00 balance.

| Date | Event | Amount | Balance after |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oct 3 | Invoice 1 | $120.00 | $120.00 |
| Oct 10 | Invoice 2 | $90.00 | $210.00 |
| Oct 12 | Payment, cash | -$150.00 | $60.00 |
| Oct 20 | Invoice 3 | $60.00 | $120.00 |

Allocation of the $150 payment: $120.00 clears Invoice 1 completely; $30.00 goes to Invoice 2, leaving $60.00 owing on it. Closing balance $120.00 = $60.00 (Invoice 2) + $60.00 (Invoice 3).

==============================================================================
## 7. Returns and Damage
==============================================================================

### 7.1 Customer returns (recorded by worker)

Fields: customer, product, packets (with their size), reason (Damaged, Quality complaint, Wrong item, Expired, Other), optional linked invoice, and a resolution:

- Credit: reduces the customer's balance at the unit price on the linked invoice, or the customer's current price if no invoice is linked.
- Replacement: free replacement packets are handed over. The balance does not change. The replacement chapathis are used up like any others and are counted in material usage (section 9). Their cost is shown on the returns list for information only.

### 7.2 Production damage

Chapathi damaged during production is entered by the Admin in chapathis (date, product, chapathis, note) and is add-only (A-7). It counts in material usage, so it raises the cost of every packet sold. It appears on the returns and damage list but is not a customer return.

### 7.3 Handling of returned goods

Returned product is half-cooked and perishable, so it is not restocked; it is a write-off (A-6). Reports show returns and damage on their own lines.

==============================================================================
## 8. Production and Stock
==============================================================================

Batch records and finished-goods stock are not part of v1 (A-8 withdrawn). The amount of each material used is calculated from what was sold, replaced and damaged (section 9), and the Admin tracks raw-material stock from purchases. A negative closing stock shows a warning, because it usually means a purchase was not entered. It never blocks a sale.

## 9. Costing
==============================================================================

### 9.1 Principles

- Cost is calculated by the system from purchases, recipe, sales and damage. It is never typed in by hand, and workers never see it.
- Both products share one list of materials, so one bag of wheat serves both.
- Missing inputs are flagged as incomplete. A missing value is never silently treated as zero.

### 9.2 Recipe basis: per 1 kg of whole wheat flour

| Material | Quantity per 1 kg wheat | Mamre Fresh Chapathi | Mamre Chapathi |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whole wheat flour | 1 kg | Yes | Yes |
| Oil | 80 ml | Yes | Yes |
| Sugar | 20 g | Yes | Yes |
| Salt | 15 g | Yes | Yes |
| Baking powder | 2 g | Yes | Yes |
| Potassium sorbate | PENDING (P-2), in grams | No | Yes |
| Yield | 32 chapathis per kg (editable) | Yes | Yes |
| Packing | 1 piece per packet, bought like any material | Yes | Yes |

### 9.3 Quantities per chapathi and per standard packet of 12

| Material | Per chapathi | Standard packet (12) |
|---|---|---|
| Whole wheat flour | 31.25 g | 375 g |
| Oil | 2.5 ml | 30 ml |
| Sugar | 0.625 g | 7.5 g |
| Salt | 0.46875 g | 5.625 g |
| Baking powder | 0.0625 g | 0.75 g |
| Potassium sorbate (Mamre Chapathi only) | PENDING (P-2) | PENDING (P-2) |
| Packing | n/a | 1 piece |

A custom packet of N chapathis uses N times the per-chapathi quantities and one packing piece.

### 9.4 Purchases and stock

- The Admin enters each purchase: material, date, quantity and unit, total paid, a note. A helper turns bags x kg per bag into kilograms. Purchases are add-only. A mistake is corrected by a reversing entry (negative quantity and amount, a required reason, linked to the original). An entry can be reversed once and a reversal cannot be reversed (A-18).
- Opening stock is entered once per material when records start; later months carry forward.
- Price per unit = weighted average = (value of opening stock + value of purchases) / (opening quantity + quantity bought) (A-10).
- Closing stock = opening stock + bought - used. A negative result shows a warning. A material that was used but has no price makes the result INCOMPLETE.

### 9.5 Usage

>> [RULE]
> Used (per material) = (chapathis invoiced + replacement chapathis + production-damaged chapathis) x recipe quantity per chapathi x (1 + wastage).
> Chapathis invoiced = packets x chapathis per packet, over non-void invoices, gross: return credits are not subtracted, because returned goods were already made. Wastage is a setting, default 2%, adjustable from 0% to 5%, and applies to ingredients only. Potassium sorbate is counted only for Mamre Chapathi.
> Packing used = packets invoiced + replacement packets, with no wastage. Damaged chapathis are lost before packing, so they use materials but no packing (A-20, A-22).

### 9.6 Calculation rules

1. Cost consumed per material = used x price per unit. Direct expense for the month = cost consumed over all ingredients + packing consumed.
2. Direct cost per packet, per product = (ingredient cost per chapathi x chapathis in the packet) + packing price per piece.
3. Indirect cost per chapathi for a month = total indirect expenses / net chapathis sold, both products together, equal per chapathi (A-9). Net chapathis = invoiced chapathis - chapathis credited through returns.
4. Full cost per packet = direct cost per packet + indirect cost per chapathi x chapathis in the packet.

### 9.7 Worked examples (illustrative prices, NOT real)

Stock example. In a month 12,000 chapathis are invoiced, replaced or damaged. Wastage is 2%.

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| Wheat needed before wastage (12,000 x 31.25 g) | 375 kg |
| Wheat used with 2% wastage | 382.5 kg |
| Bought: 400 kg for $440.00 | $1.10 per kg |
| Cost consumed (382.5 kg x $1.10) | $420.75 |
| Closing stock (400 - 382.5) | 17.5 kg |

Cost of a standard packet of 12 chapathis (Mamre Fresh Chapathi, before wastage). Invented prices: wheat $1.10/kg, oil $4.00/L, sugar $1.00/kg, salt $0.80/kg, baking powder $4.00/kg; monthly indirect expenses $900 over 72,000 net chapathis ($0.0125 each).

## 10. Expenses
==============================================================================

### 10.1 Direct expenses

Direct expense is the cost of the materials used (wheat, oil, sugar, salt, baking powder, potassium sorbate) plus packing, calculated from purchases and usage as described in section 9. It replaces the earlier recipe-times-batch method (A-11 withdrawn).

### 10.2 Indirect expenses

Categories at launch: Electricity, Water, Machine, Labour, Maintenance, Delivery charge, Fuel, Other. An entry has: date, category, amount, description. The Admin logs expenses at first; worker logging of fuel and delivery is a later version. Machine cost is entered as a monthly amount (rent, lease or an amortised share) (A-12).

Expense entries are add-only. A mistake is corrected by a reversing entry with a reason, as for purchases (A-18). The Admin can add categories.

## 11. Reports (Admin Only)
==============================================================================

The monthly report is available on screen and as PDF and Excel. It contains:

1. Sales summary: packets, chapathis and revenue by product and by customer type (Restaurant, Shop, Retail, Catering); invoiced total, credits, net sales.
2. Collections: cash received in the month by method; outstanding balance at month end.
3. Direct expense and gross profit.
4. Materials: a monthly table per material with opening stock, bought, used, closing stock, average price and cost consumed, with warnings for negative stock and missing prices.
5. Indirect expenses by category, with the total.
6. Net profit.
7. Customer balances with ageing (current, 30+ days, 60+ days).
8. Returns and damage: customer returns, replacement cost (for information) and production damage.
9. Cost per chapathi and per standard packet per product: direct, indirect share, full cost.
10. Price changes: invoice lines where the charged price differs from the list price.
11. Activity per worker: invoices and totals.

>> [PROFIT DEFINITIONS]
> Net sales = non-void invoices - return credits.
> Direct expense = cost consumed of all materials + packing consumed (section 9).
> Gross profit = net sales - direct expense.
> Net profit = gross profit - indirect expenses.
> Cash collected = payments received in the month (a separate cash view; it never replaces the sales view).
> Production damage, wastage and replacement packets are already inside direct expense, so they are shown for information and are never subtracted a second time.

## 14. Glossary
==============================================================================

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Chapathi | One half-cooked chapathi. Material usage is calculated per chapathi. |
| Packet | The sales unit: a standard packet (12 chapathis) or a custom packet (1 to 200). |
| Custom packet | A packet with a chosen number of chapathis, available for every customer type. |
| Credit customer | A customer billed monthly who may carry a balance. |
| Walk-in | An anonymous Retail sale paid in full at once, with no ledger. |
| Override price | An Admin-set price for one customer and product, taking priority over the default. |
| Worker can edit price | A per-customer-type switch that lets the worker change a line's price. |
| Void | Admin cancellation of an invoice. The number stays and shows VOID. |
| Reversing entry | A linked negative entry, with a reason, that corrects a purchase or expense without editing it. |
| Credit (return) | A reduction of a customer's balance because of returned goods. |
| Net sales | Invoiced total less return credits. |
| Direct expense | Cost of materials used plus packing for the month. |
| Indirect expense | Electricity, water, machine, labour, maintenance, delivery, fuel and similar. |
| Ledger | The chronological record of a customer's invoices, credits and payments. |
